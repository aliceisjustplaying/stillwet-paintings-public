# The rounds, in plain words

Each round is one experiment. The painters are AI models. Each one paints by writing code against a simulation of oil paint on linen and, from October 12, of watercolor on paper. The early rounds painted after Caspar David Friedrich; later studios were made for Hopper (round 20), Inness (22.1), Monet (26) and Cézanne (39.1), and some rounds left the subject to the painter. The painters work from written notes, never from pictures; round 22's studio for Kendric Tonn was to hold pictures of his work, but its only painter ran without them. Dates are 2026, UTC. Sources are paths in the claude-paint repository unless they say otherwise.

**Round 1 (September 22).** Three painters who had never seen a painting made with the simulator each composed an original picture: a winter, a coast and a mountain view. All three found Friedrich-like pictures: a cross among firs, a shore at dusk with a brig, a winter cross with a dead oak and a church in fog. The winter was judged best. Their skies were too straight and too alike, and the paint dried darker than they meant. A power loss ended the sessions before the painters could finish. *(notes/fresh_painters.md)*

**Round 2 (September 23, night).** New painters took the same three themes, with a short brief and an improved simulator. They had no access to round 1. All three finished in under an hour. The skies became smooth, different from each other and free of ruled strokes, and the mist and distance got better. The winter picture, *Winter Evening with a Ruined Choir*, became the project's benchmark. Later human and AI judges kept ranking it first. *(notes/amnesia2.md; notes/round11/blind/key.md)*

**Round 3 (September 23).** Painting at an easel, live: the painters laid down paint a passage at a time, stepped back to look and undid what failed. One painter chose its own subject, one was asked for a daylight summer scene and one for a close-up subject. The free painter chose the same twilight, crescent moon and figure seen from behind as round 2. The steered painters chose other subjects. The painters said the looking and undoing "felt like painting" and that drawing shapes felt like programming. *(notes/amnesia3.md)*

**Round 4 (September 23).** The easel again, with the same three assignments, now with drawing tools: a pencil underdrawing, hand-drawn outlines and masks for what is in front and what is behind. These tools answered round 3's complaint that the painters couldn't draw. *(notes/round4_plan.md)*

**Rounds 5 and 6 (no whole new paintings here).** Round 5 had painters rework two existing pictures in short loops, with critics scoring each version. Round 6 was a lab of single-subject studies (a bare tree, foliage, a rock, a sky, water), each painted the old way and the new way for Alice to compare. Her yardstick came from this round: "less digital and more bad painter." *(notes/round5_plan.md; notes/round6/alice_review.md)*

**Round 7 (September 24).** Three ways of working were compared, each painter choosing its own subject: the easel with no ready-made shapes, one hand-written program and the easel with every tool. A fourth painter wrote a plain Python program with no paint simulation at all, as a control. Left free, the three painters nearly converged on one picture: calm water, an evening sky and trees reflected in it. None of them moved Alice the way round 2's winter had. *(notes/round7/arms/; notes/round6/alice_review.md)*

**Round 8 (September 24–25, overnight).** The painters got a commission, as a patron would give one: "a winter landscape." They used round 2's style of brief and the same three ways of working. Every picture had figures, a route through the snow and motifs the painters drew themselves. One blind AI critic ranked the wayside-cross picture first, above round 2's winter. *(notes/round8/blind/)*

**Round 9 (September 25, overnight).** A repeat of round 8 with two lines added to the brief: paint skies in broad strokes blended while wet, not a speckled veil, and let the surrounding paint bury the feet of things. The critics said the painted-on cracks of age were now the weakest part. *(notes/round9/)*

**Round 10 (September 25).** Round 2 again on a newer engine: its brief word for word, one program per painting and the same model at high reasoning. There were two winters and one green summer. Alice: "This feels like progress." The person whose online post started the project said of the summer lime tree: "The trees are amazing." *(notes/round10/; notes/round6/alice_review.md)*

**Round 11 (September 25).** Other AI models painted the same winter brief: OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra, Google's Gemini 3.8 Flash and Anthropic's Claude Fable 5.1. Gemini's painting is unfinished because it ran out of credits. Each model then judged all six winters blind, its own included. Each ranked its own picture fifth or sixth, and all three ranked round 2's winter first. *(notes/round11/)*

**Round 12 (September 25).** A study of one bare tree in winter, grown with the simulator's model of how trees branch. Alice found the results "very fractal-ey, which is the opposite of what we are going for." *(notes/round12/; notes/round6/alice_review.md)*

**Round 13 (September 25).** The same tree study with the growth model taken away. Each painter placed its trunk and limbs by hand and wrote its own rules for the fine branches. Alice: "Ouch. Wow. Ouch. U is the least bad." *(notes/round13/; notes/round6/alice_review.md)*

**Round 14 (September 25–26).** A chain of painters. Each painted a landscape of its own choosing and wrote craft notes for the next. The first attempt read a notebook of earlier painters' experience as a set of instructions, and painted a plowed field that Alice found "very funny ... adorable" but "not a Friedrich". The chain was restarted and gave a mountain morning, an evening town on a coastal plain and a Baltic shore at dusk. *(notes/round14/; notes/round6/alice_review.md)*

**Round 15 (September 26).** The chain again, from a stripped-down studio holding only the tools and notes a painter needs. The pictures: misty mountains, an oak in snow with a church and a Baltic shore. All three are set at dusk or dawn, with a crescent moon and a figure seen from behind. The third painter repainted round 14's shore almost exactly, without having seen it. *(notes/round15/)*

**Round 16 (September 27).** Four chains of three painters worked at the easel with no undo, no previews and paint mixed by hand from the tubes. Between painters, a reader wrote notes on what the paint and tools did, with no subjects. Chains A (winter) and B (summer) painted after Friedrich; chains C and D had a free subject and no Friedrich. A, B and C were Claude Opus; D was Google's Gemini, whose first painter stopped before a final render; a saved view survives as a study. The first two winter painters chose the same title, *Hünengrab im Schnee am Abend*, without seeing each other's work. All three summer titles begin "Summer Evening". Left free, one Opus painter and both remaining Gemini painters painted a still life with a jug; the other two Opus painters went back to evening waterscapes. *(notes/round16/look/README.md; notes/HANDOFF.md)*

**Round 17 (September 27).** Two chains of three Claude Opus painters at the easel, each painter working in up to four sittings, each a new session returning to the same canvas. Chain F painted after Friedrich, a landscape on a June day; chain O had a subject of its own choosing. The first two F painters painted June mornings on river meadows, under the same title; all three O painters chose an estuary at evening. Varnish and cracks were applied after each painter's last session, on a replay of its log, not by the painters. *(runs/gallery-fcf9c110/r17/r17_chains.py; r17/run/)*

**Round 18 (September 27).** One painter per model, six models, a free subject: four painted still lifes and two painted evening water, a marsh and an estuary.

**Round 19 (September 27–29).** One painter per model, each alone at the easel in up to four sittings, with only the easel's own tools: paint, look, keep a journal and read the studio's notes. GPT-6 Luna, Gemini 3.8 Flash, Kimi K3, Muse Spark 1.3, Claude Sonnet 5.5 and GPT-6.1 Sol painted after Friedrich; Luna and Gemini also painted a subject of their own choosing, and Claude Sonnet 5 two self-portraits. All six Friedrich paintings have a bare tree. Gemini's two paintings stopped when its API credits ran out, and Kimi's when the provider's usage limit did not lift for a day. Varnish and cracks were applied after each painter's last sitting, on a replay of its log. *(runs/gallery-fcf9c110/r19/r19_chains.py; r19/run/chains.log)*

**Round 20 (September 29).** Studios for painting in the manner of other painters, each with a tube box of the pigments its painter is documented using. One painter ran: GPT-6.1 Sol, on the day it launched, in a studio for Edward Hopper, asked for a scene of American city life. It painted a cleaners' shop window in afternoon sun. *(runs/gallery-fcf9c110/r20/r20_chains.py; claude-paint notes/r20/BUILD.md)*

**Round 21.5 (October 2).** Claude Sonnet 5.5 after Friedrich again, as in round 19, at the end of five prompt changes made one after another (rounds 21.1 to 21.5): the brief in a file, a line saying it was not being evaluated in a quantifiable way, no counters, no machine minutes or costs and tree notes no longer leading with an old, stag-headed oak. Rounds 21.1 to 21.4 were stopped early in their first sitting. The painter still painted a large bare oak. *(claude-paint notes/handoff-2026-10-02.md; notes/round21.5/runner/)*

**Round 22 (October 2–3).** A studio for Kendric Tonn, the first to hold pictures of the artist's work, there with his permission. Until then painters had never seen pictures of the artist's work. Claude Fable 5.1 was to paint with them, but only the warm-up ran: Space Bunny, in Tonn's studio as it was before the pictures. It painted a still life of four quinces, a bowl and a dark bottle, gave it a new title at every sitting and stopped at the four-sitting limit as *Quinces, Raking Light*. In its own words: "Tonn's fields are built of visible standing strokes — mine are laid down and smoothed." The picture isn't on the site. *(notes/round22/runner/CHANGES.md; notes/round22/runner/run/BUNT/)*

**Round 22.1 (October 2).** The first painter in a studio for George Inness, with his box and a note on his materials: Claude Opus 5.5 at extra high reasoning. It painted for 80 minutes, then 12, and ended when a third sitting added no painting. The picture is a late-day pastoral: a dark grove of oaks whose long shadow lies across the meadow and one warm accent, "a small figure in a red shawl". In the last sitting its title changed from *Evening, the Long Shadow* to *Evening, the Edge of the Grove*. It stopped there: "Every time I tried a local repair on dry paint in this painting, it left a visible seam, so I've stopped while the large shapes still hold together." *(notes/round22.1/runner/CHANGES.md; notes/round22.1/runner/run/INNS/)*

**Round 24 (October 4).** Round 23 had run the Inness studio again with one new view of the palette; its painter stopped after about 70 minutes, with no reason recorded. Round 24 moved the studio to engine 3: a rag to lift wet paint, oil paint that stays open and dries on a clock taken from the sources and no rebuild after a failed chunk. At Alice's request the brief added "You may sign the painting," and the painter was shown live in the website's studio and on the stream. Claude Fable 5.1 painted *Evening Pool, After Haying* in one 48-minute sitting: "A late-summer meadow at sundown, in the tonal manner of late Inness," with a small pool and a tiny figure in a red shawl. Its monogram "reads as "T F.", which stands for nothing." Its second sitting was one look at the canvas, eight seconds long, and the runner accepted that as finishing. *(r23/CHANGES.md; r24/CHANGES.md; notes/round24/LAUNCH.md; notes/round24/retrospective/RETRO.md)*

**Round 24.3 (October 4–5).** Three prompt changes for the Inness studio within 12 minutes (rounds 24.1 to 24.3). Round 24.1 asked for a fresh look at the whole picture and its details before the painter called it finished. Round 24.2 switched to Claude Opus 5.5 and told it to take its time: "Ask yourself: is your heart happy with this?" Round 24.3 opened with "Invent a place you would enjoy painting," with Inness's methods to come after. Rounds 24.1 and 24.2 were stopped after a minute or two. In 24.3 Opus painted a river meadow about twenty minutes after sunset, with a woman in a red shawl as "the one warm note in the picture." It declared itself satisfied, made more repairs each time it was brought back and stopped at the four-sitting limit, marked not finished. Its title changed at every sitting and ended as *October Dusk, the River Meadow*. *(tags round-24.1 to round-24.3; r24.3/run/; notes/round24/retrospective/RETRO.md)*

**Round 25 (October 5).** Friedrich again, on engine 5, with a box of 16 tubes: the original fourteen plus Naples yellow and rose madder. The opening added "Let winter be the season." There was no limit on sittings; a painter was done when it looked over the whole picture and its details in a sitting and added no paint. Claude Opus 5.5 painted *The Oak in Snow at Evening* in six sittings, 78 minutes in all: "one old oak, set on the central axis at the top of a shallow snow-covered rise, just after sunset." A crescent moon and a small crow came later. In the last sitting it tried grass stalks in the foreground and wiped them: "The trial made it clear that the empty foreground is a choice, not a gap." Varnish and cracks were applied after the session. *(notes/round25/LAUNCH.md; r25/run/FRDC/)*

**Round 26 (October 5).** A studio for painting in the manner of later Claude Monet, with his 15-tube Giverny box and a reviewed note on his materials. The brief added: "Keep exploring what interests you." Claude Opus 5.5 painted *Nymphéas, reflet du soir* in three sittings, 51 minutes in all: a lily pond seen straight down with no horizon, two willow reflections hanging from the top corners and "a band of evening sky" coming down through the water. Its last words: "My heart is happy with this. The light runs down the pond like a river of sky between the two willows." The runner logged the last sitting as adding no painting. A later check found brush gestures and rag wipes in it that the runner didn't count, and that was fixed. *(notes/round26/LAUNCH.md; notes/round26/RETROSPECTIVE.md; notes/round26/BUG_FIXES.md)*

**Round 27 (October 5).** The Hopper studio with its 13-tube box and one new paragraph in the brief: "After the first large masses and each major campaign, inspect the whole picture in normal color against your intended effect." Claude Opus 5.5 painted *Barber Shop, Late Afternoon*: a brick corner building in late-afternoon sun and a man "in a white shirt and a straw hat" tilted back on a kitchen chair beside the barber pole. It signed it "Claude". It worked three sittings, 33 minutes in all, and the last added no paint. The review afterward: "Its main limitation is still graphic construction followed by repeated local repair." *(notes/round27/LAUNCH.md; notes/round27/RETROSPECTIVE.md; r27/run/HOPR/)*

**Round 28 (October 5).** The Hopper studio with a longer set of working instructions, opening "Work one campaign at a time. After laying a campaign, inspect the whole picture in normal color and relevant detail before writing the code for the next campaign." Claude Opus 5.5 planned a late-afternoon corner with a ground-floor lunchroom and drew it in pencil. About three minutes in, Alice switched the round to Monet and the painter was stopped. The drawing is kept as a study. *(notes/round28/LAUNCH.md; notes/round28/readiness/stopped.json)*

**Round 29 (October 5).** Round 28's working instructions, word for word, in the Monet studio. Claude Opus 5.5 painted *Evening Gold, Willow Pool*: "a pond with no horizon," reflections of two weeping willows hanging from the top edge and between them "a column of gold and rose evening sky," with seven islands of lily pads. Most of it came in an 84-minute first sitting; short sittings followed until one added no paint. It was streamed live. It stopped because "more touches would start to fuss a picture whose strength is its softness." *(notes/round29/LAUNCH.md; r29/run/MONT/)*

**Round 30 (October 5).** The same instructions back in the Hopper studio. Claude Opus 5.5 painted *Luncheonette, Four O'Clock*: a brick corner building in low afternoon sun, a counterman in a white coat in the doorway and a woman leaning on the sill of the one open upstairs window. "Each is alone, and neither knows the other is there." It worked "the way Hopper did: a pencil drawing, a lay-in thinned almost entirely with turpentine, then opaque body paint laid wet over dry." Four sittings, 55 minutes in all; the last added no paint. Later briefs copy this round's template word for word. *(r30/r21_chains.py; r30/run/HOPR/)*

**Round 31 (October 5–6).** A studio with a free subject and every tube in the catalog, 54 colors. The brief opened: "Paint one picture of your choosing in oil … Subject, composition and manner are yours." The planned round never ran. A test export of its studio was painted to the end: Claude Opus 5.5 planned a "grainstack at sunset, after Monet" and painted *Grainstack, Evening* over nine sittings, the last adding no paint. Its journal ends: "my heart is happy. That seam was the last mechanical edge I could find, and now nothing in the picture shows how it was made more than it shows light. Finished at day 415." *(r31/r21_chains.py; r31-smoke/run/; notes/journal-mining/yolo-r31-smoke-journal.md)*

**Round 32 (October 6–8).** Round 31's studio with one new sentence in the brief: "Choose a subject you would want to look at, not one a famous painter is known for." Claude Opus 5.5 planned "a street fruit stall at night lit by one bare bulb under a canvas awning." An hour in, the easel stopped answering: a three-day wait on the painting's clock over a thinned veil was taking hours of real time. The engine was fixed, the easel reopened from its log and the painter finished on October 8: *Fruit Stall After Dark*, a woman in a red hat reaching for the lemons beside a blue night street. "Anything more now would be fiddling, so I stopped. My heart is happy with it." *(notes/round32/BRIEF-draft.md; r32/run/FREE/; commit 54ef1bf6)*

**Round 33 (October 6–7).** The Friedrich studio, with no season given, for a newly released model: Mistral Large 4, through OpenRouter. In the first attempt Mistral refused requests carrying more than ten pictures. The second attempt, on a fresh canvas, painted 25 chunks of a lake at dusk with a figure and a bare tree in a 150-minute sitting. Every sitting after that failed because the account had run out of credits, and the runner stopped after six. Neither attempt is on the site. *(r33/r21_chains.py; r33/run/chains.log)*

**Round 34 (October 7).** The free-subject studio with all 54 tubes, its opening word for word as in round 31's test, without round 32's sentence about famous painters. The painter was Claude Haiku 5.5 at maximum reasoning. It planned a coastal dusk with wet sand and painted *Evening Light on Still Water*: "A calm sunset over a violet sea," a pale sun on the horizon and "a glitter path" running down the water. It worked five sittings, 172 minutes in all. After the fourth, the runner didn't count it as done, because a read-only query came after its last look. The fifth ended it: "I'm content with the picture. The headland was the change I most wanted, and it's done." *(r34/r21_chains.py; r34/run/FREE/)*

**Round 35 (October 7).** Round 34 exactly, with OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra at high reasoning. In 11 chunks and nine minutes it painted *Two Embers and a Blue Jug*: "A cool blue vessel anchors folded linen and two glowing persimmons against a quiet violet wall." A second sitting looked again and left it unchanged. Minutes before, two short Astra sessions with the same brief had each planned a blue jug or pitcher with orange fruit and stopped within three minutes; no record says why. One of them is kept as a study. *(r35/r21_chains.py; r35/run/FREE/)*

**Round 36 (October 7).** GPT-6 Astra, as in round 35, in the Friedrich studio. In a 34-minute sitting it painted *The Gate of the Silent Lake*: "An empty stone arch frames pale mountains beyond motionless water—a threshold without a path." Its plan had no figure: "the empty aperture is the object of contemplation." A second short sitting added two chunks and a third added nothing. Varnish and cracks were applied after the session. *(r36/r21_chains.py; r36/run/FRDC/)*

**Round 37 (October 7).** GPT-6 Astra in the Hopper studio. In 11 minutes it painted *Westbound, Sunday*: "A lone traveler waits behind glass while sunlight divides an otherwise empty city street." Three more sittings of a minute or two added five chunks. The runner turned down its first "done" for the same reason as round 34's. *(r37/r21_chains.py; r37/run/HOPR/)*

**Round 38 (October 7).** GPT-6 Astra in the Monet studio. In a 24-minute sitting it painted *The Hour Held in the Pool*: "Pink lilies interrupt a lilac reflection between green curtains of willow." From the third sitting on it said it was done ("I'm happy to leave it unchanged"), but each time it printed drying times after its last look, so the runner kept bringing it back. After twelve sittings it was marked finished by hand. *(r38/r21_chains.py; r38/run/MONT/p1_outcome.json)*

**Round 39.1 (October 10).** A new studio for Paul Cézanne, with a box of the eleven tubes he is documented using. Before launch, an opening that named apples, a jug and a cloth was replaced with Alice's unrestricted one, and a note recommending a first still life was cut. In round 39 Claude Opus 5.5 planned a still life and was stopped after about three minutes. Round 39.1 changed one phrase of the brief to "one original open-air scene." The same model painted *The Village below the Mountain, with Two Pines* in six sittings, 67 minutes in all, "almost everything … built from patches of short, parallel flat-brush strokes." It was streamed live. "My heart is happy with it, and I can't see anything I'd enjoy taking further." *(r39/r21_chains.py; r39.1/run/CEZN/; drive handoffs/cezanne-studio/)*

**Round 40 (October 10).** The Hopper studio again, with "night" added to the brief's first sentence and a new section of the materials note on night and artificial light. Rounds 27, 30 and 37 had all painted daytime. Claude Opus 5.5 painted *Cleaners, Late*, a dark corner where one shop is lit, a dry cleaner's under fluorescent tubes: "The idea is Hopper's: one lit interior as the only warm thing in a dark, quiet city, with someone in it working alone." During the first sitting the computer's disk filled and the easel refused every tool. The last chunk was lost, the canvas was restored to the 72 chunks before it and the painter went on for three more sittings. It was streamed live. *(r40/r21_chains.py; r40/run/chains.log; drive handoffs/r40-hopper/)*
