# The painter's last reply

**After Harvest, Storm Coming**

A harvested field under a slate storm sky: furrowed gold stubble half in cloud shadow, three hay stooks, and a pale track curving to a small white farmhouse tucked into a dark wood, one window already lit. Rooks cross the sky while light still breaks at the horizon, and a dark oak at the right edge frames the view.

This sitting I gave the whole canvas and detail crops of every previously unresolved passage a fresh look, then judged the one campaign that had been left mid-flight: a single graded pass repainting the entire wood as one mass. It worked — the three separate lighter oval crowns that used to float on the tree line are gone, light now falls continuously across the wood, and after eight days' drying (day 75, dry everywhere) the passage holds exactly as it looked wet, with no seam reappearing. That was the one passage still pulling against the picture; with it resolved, my heart is happy with the painting in its settled, stylised folk-painterly voice, and I am stopping rather than reopening repair loops that repeatedly made passages worse.

**Handoff — imperfections deliberately accepted:** a faint seam and pale bar in the upper sky, reading as cloud strata at whole scale; the farmhouse, graphic at full-detail crop but a clear focal jewel at its actual scale; the oak's straight framing-post trunk with a reddish ghost that reads as trunk seen through leaves; the rounded tree behind the oak trunk and one small wind-tossed tuft above the middle crown; coarse shadow dabs at bottom left that carry the furrow weight at whole scale.

**Lessons carried forward:** bounded patch masks leave cut seams — repaint whole regions in one graded pass (the wood fix: one body pass over the unified wood mask, opaque earth-and-Prussian green graded to dark below, lit green capped at the tops, stipple into the wet paint, no overall blend); a soft mask edge does not soften `work()` output — use `edge=` or graded piles; turps washes over pale ground dry far lighter than masstone while body paint on dry layers hits darker and more saturated, so judge mixtures by small laid trials on the intended substrate and wetness; repainting over still-open paint marbled the crowns whereas the same recipe covered solidly once dry — check `drying()` and wait; a spirits-dipped rag rescues a failed wet campaign over dry paint but smears into neighbours unless masked away; stipple and hatch stay separate on dry paint but fuse usefully into wet paint.

**Failed recipes not to retry unchanged:** fine hatch stubble on the dry field; gesture-fan and ribbon-sheaf stooks; undiluted filbert-24 cloud gestures on dry sky; a viridian glaze at medium 0.55 with the long default glaze hand; `lose()` to soften track edges; clipped sky "carves" against a canopy; reusing the original sky mask after the tree silhouette outgrew it; and repainting crowns inside their old ellipse mask, which only preserves the disc.
