# The painter's last reply

**Fruit Stall After Dark** is finished. The painting clock is still at day 363; I didn't lay any paint this sitting.

I looked at the whole canvas and close-ups of the fruit, the figure, the awning and bulb, and the street. Last sitting's repair has held now that it's dry: the window reflections in the wet road are still clean orange and gold dashes fading downward, with no trace of the old green. The light still gathers at the bare bulb. The woman in the red hat stands dark against the lemons, and the blue street sits calm beside the warm stall.

**Flaws I'm leaving as they are:**
- **Right-hand lemons:** they keep a faint peel-like ridge texture, but the brown crackle hasn't come back.
- **Pomegranates:** the swirling brushwork reads as texture in the shade.
- **Coat:** the yellow flecks read as caught light.
- **Fruit:** some heaps still sit in loose rows.
- **Counter front:** the swirling strokes read as wood grain.
- **New this time:** a few red and cream flecks sit just above the awning's top edge. They read as a frayed edge catching the light. Tidying them with a dark line would make a hard, ruled edge.
- **Blue pillar:** it's flat, but it works as the dark frame between the warm stall and the night street.

Anything more now would be fiddling, so I stopped. My heart is happy with it.

The journal has the updated handoff, with the practical lessons unchanged:
1. Repaint whole passages in one pass.
2. Cure a hard edge where a shape stops by painting new shapes across it.
3. Lay glazes in nested zones.
4. A spirit rag lifts thin, still-wet glaze; a knife scrape takes off a fresh thick layer.
5. Repeated identical modelling marks read as a pattern.
6. Put light fruit in shade with darker paint plus a golden glaze.
7. Paint reflections in wet ground as vertical drags of the object's own colour.
8. Paint warm reflections on a blue ground as thick, unblended dashes over dry paint.
9. Keep helpers global, and re-sign if a repaint covers the signature.
