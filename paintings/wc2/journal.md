  Practical lessons (hypotheses unless noted):
  - Value concentration: small dark marks need 1–3 pigment units per uL; washes 0.02–0.06 per uL. Concentration, not volume, drives darkness (observed on scratch).
  - Long wet rows: top up every 30–60 cells to a fixed load; single long loaded strokes empty early (observed).
  - Pre-wet drop grids + many rows over large dry painted areas can flood and migrate ~100 cells beyond the strokes, drying with cauliflower edges (observed once on painting; scratch trial didn't reproduce — condition difference unknown, maybe the painting had more surrounding glazes/less absorbency or more total water). Keep pre-wet away from finished focal objects.
  - Opaque light streaks (buff titanium) on dark mud = collage look; opaque lights work as thin vertical grass and boat rail (observed).
  - Cadmium red over dark dried paint stays bright (observed); indian red reads salmon/chalky.
  - Stacked fading rows from a crisp top line make convincing mud ridges/sandbars (observed).
