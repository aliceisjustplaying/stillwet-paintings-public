# The painter's last reply

**Low Water, Evening Channel**

A tidal estuary at dusk. A pale channel curves from the lit horizon down to the bottom of the sheet through mudflats, and a line of old posts runs beside it into the distance. A small red boat sits on the mud below a dark bank of marsh, and a wooded headland stands against a violet-grey sky with a band of yellow glow along the horizon. The sky was painted wet-in-wet over a light pre-wet, with soft clouds added while it was still damp. The mud is built from glazes of warm, grainy paint, with sandbars and the deep foreground laid over them, and the boat's red is the one strong colour in the picture.

**Unresolved passages:**
- On the left, the middle-distance mud still shows stacked horizontal strokes that look like rows of bricks.
- The dark foreground is heavy and has faint horizontal stripes.
- **An accident in the foreground:** a wet glaze there spread about 100 cells up the sheet and dried with a ragged, cauliflower-like top edge. It also dulled the boat. I kept that edge as a bank of marsh plants and repainted the boat's red, but it was not planned.
- The tree line is quite even, like a fence.
- The lower channel is a flat lavender, and the posts and boat have no reflections in the water.
- The signature, a small "C" in the lower right, barely shows on the dark mud.

**Lessons, in more detail in the journal:**
- **Darkness depends on concentration.** Small dark marks needed about 1–3 pigment units per µL of water, while broad washes needed about 0.02–0.06. How much water the brush held mattered much less.
- **Long rows need refilling.** A long wash row needs topping up every 30–60 cells; one heavily loaded stroke runs out early.
- **A pre-wet can spread far.** A grid of water drops followed by many rows over dry painted mud ran far beyond the strokes. The same recipe stayed in place on the scratch sheet and I don't know why, so I treat this as a hypothesis. For now I'd keep pre-wetting away from finished focal objects.
- **Opaque lights:** streaks of buff titanium on dark mud looked pasted on and had to be painted over. The same opaque lights worked as thin upright grass blades and for the boat's rail.
- **Red over dark:** cadmium red stays bright over dark dried paint, while indian red looked chalky and salmon-coloured.
- **Sandbars:** stacked rows that start from a crisp curved top line and fade row by row made convincing mud ridges.
