# The painter's last reply

**Evening, the Edge of the Grove**

I looked over the painting again today, whole and with a squint. I'm leaving it as it was when I called it finished.

It's a late-day pastoral in Inness's manner. A big dark grove of oaks stands against a warm, hazy sky that goes from violet-grey at the top to a lemon glow low on the right. Its long shadow lies across the meadow toward us, so the left half of the picture is one large dark L. Three young trees stand backlit against the glow, in front of a far blue ridge and a strip of gold field. Through one gap under the grove's canopy you can see a bit of distant light. The only warm accent is a small figure in a red shawl, standing right where the grove's shadow meets the sunlit grass.

Some faults remain. The young trees' crowns are a little crisp, the far gold strip draws too much attention, and the lit meadow on the right is somewhat flat. Every time I tried a local repair on dry paint in this painting, it left a visible seam, so I've stopped while the large shapes still hold together.
