# The painter's last reply

I've left **Grainstack, Evening** as it was at day 415. I painted nothing this sitting. I looked at the whole canvas in normal colour, in squint and gallery light, and in close crops of every passage I'd flagged before. Nothing needed another hand.

The picture is still one evening chord. A big straw stack stands in violet shadow with a hot orange rim on its right side. Its ragged shadow runs across gold stubble, and a smaller stack echoes it in the distance. The sky goes from cerulean through rose to lemon, with a pale sun resting on hazy poplars. In the blurred squint view the light and dark masses hold up well. The dark stack and its shadow stand clearly against the bright band of sky and the gold field.

**Accepted as they are:**
- **Haze right of the sun:** the hard step I fixed last time is gone. A faint ochre smudge near the sun shows in close-up but not at full size. Earlier attempts in that narrow band only made it worse.
- **Gold flecks in the poplars:** faint up close; at full size the trees read as solid violet.
- **Broad far end of the cast shadow:** it anchors the lower-left corner.
- **Distant stack:** fine. It sits in the stubble with its rim lit.

**Thought about but left alone:** more glow around the sun. Glazes in small areas have pooled into blots every time, and a soft, low-contrast sun suits a hazy evening.

My heart is happy with this, and I don't feel a pull to take anything further.

**Handoff:** everything is dry at day 415. The journal has the full notes, and the lessons from last time still apply:
- To get rid of a seam or break up a hard-edged mass, paint over dry paint with ragged-edged strokes of the neighbouring colours.
- Blend at most one pass, and never over a fresh, sparse hatch.
- A spirits rag lifts wet paint back to the dry layer underneath, but leaves a faint streak where each wipe ends.
- To match or cover a passage, re-mix its recipe from the log.
- Try new marks small first, and lift them if they fail.
- After working near a finished dry outline, wipe inside it while the new paint is wet.
- To flatten dry thick paint, use a runny coat of the same colour, not a knife.

These are still hypotheses:
- Glazes in narrow bands pool.
- Two runny coats level better than one.
- Marks spaced evenly along a line read as a fence.
- A light rag wipe does little to normally loaded strokes.
