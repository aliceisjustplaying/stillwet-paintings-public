# The painter's last reply

**Evening Light on Still Water**

A calm sunset over a violet sea. The sky runs from blue at the top through lilac and rose cloud bands to gold at the horizon. A pale sun sits on the horizon with a warm bloom, and a glitter path runs down the water toward the viewer. The headland on the left is lit on its sun-facing slope and dark on its shadow side. A small sailboat with its reflection sits on the glow at right. The sea reads as water across its width, with horizontal glints in the middle distance and larger ones near the viewer.

This sitting I made no marks on the painting. I checked the whole picture in value and colour and inspected each open passage at 1:1, and I recorded a judgement for each in the journal. I tested one repair for the upper-left sky streak on the scratch, a thin lighter wash over the reproduced grain. It softened the grain but dried as a pale patch with its own boundary, so I rejected it. The top-right cloud block's left wall is still the weakest large shape. Every cover on that block has added a shape, so I accepted it as a cloud bank edge.

**Handoff (also in the journal)**

- **Accepted, with reasons:** the upper-left streak (a weave artifact, invisible at whole view); the top-right block (a cloud bank, already covered more than twice); the slate flecks; the sun and its warm reflection; the sheen oval (a shadow streak); the lit headland; the lower-left near glints; the path foot; the sailboat.
- **Practical lessons:**
  - A lighter thin wash over a grained blue sky dries as a pale patch, not as sky.
  - Some look crops return only a file path; re-run the crop before judging it.
  - A graded re-lay of a dry mass reads as form (observed once, on the headland; untested on the top-right block).
  - Glints on the dry sea (flat 3, load .3, pressure .07–.10, horizontal) read as water.
- **Uncertain:** whether a value-matched, cooler wash would dissolve the streak; whether a graded re-lay would soften the top-right wall; whether the lower-left pills would read dimmer on the painting.

I'm content with the picture. The headland was the change I most wanted, and it's done. I'm finishing here.
