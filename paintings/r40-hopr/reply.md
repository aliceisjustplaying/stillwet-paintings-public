# The painter's last reply

**Title: *Cleaners, Late***

I didn't make any new marks this sitting, so the painting's clock is still at day 42. I looked again at the whole canvas, in normal colour and under the gallery light, and at full-size close-ups of the shop interior, the sidewalk and street glow around the hydrant, and the dark building across the side street.

**What I'm accepting as it is**
I checked each passage I'd left unresolved and recorded my judgement in the journal:
- **The diagonal edge of the street glow:** it reads as the edge of the cast light. Last sitting's test on the scratch canvas suggested repainting would only make it cloudier.
- **The soft unevenness inside the sidewalk light:** it reads as worn concrete.
- **The empty dark right third:** that's where the loneliness sits.
- **The tiny figure without features**, as in Hopper.
- **The lit upper room:** it is still clearly second to the shop.
- **A faint pale curl in one window of the dark building (new):** it only shows under the gallery light, and at normal viewing it reads as a reflection in the glass.

I thought about adding more light or shadow to the hydrant. It already has a lit left edge and a short shadow toward the curb, and it stands right where the light ends, so more would just be fussing.

**About the picture**
A brick corner at night. A dry cleaner's lit by fluorescent tubes is the one pale, cool room in it. A woman in teal stands at the counter with her hands on a folded shirt. The window throws three splayed panels of light across the sidewalk, crossed by the shadows of the window frames, and a faint glow out into the street. Above, one warm room glows behind a half-drawn shade, and across the dark side street a blue-black apartment block keeps a few windows lit. My heart is happy with it, and I've stopped here.

**Handoff**
These practical lessons still apply:
- Lay graded paint along the light's direction, then blend once while it's still wet.
- Repaint only over dry paint.
- Don't grade a passage with the rag.
- Do bars and edges with a narrow flat brush, kept inside the shape's outline, on dry paint.
- To remove a speck, use small touches of the surrounding colour on dry paint, and check at full size, because they tend to land slightly off.
- Two guesses, each tested only once: the softening tool leaves broken flecks on dry paint, and dull shade mixes look lighter on a dark wall than they do on the palette.
