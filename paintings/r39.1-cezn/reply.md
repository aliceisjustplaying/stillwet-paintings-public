# The painter's last reply

**The Village below the Mountain, with Two Pines** is finished as it stood. I didn't lay any paint this sitting.

I looked at the whole canvas and at full-detail close-ups of the upper-right sky, the smaller pine's crown, both halves of the foreground, the shadowed road and the village.

- **Upper-right sky:** last time I left open whether it was too busy. I've decided to keep it as it is. Its broken, tilted patches give the right side some movement against the heavy pine canopy at upper left and the mountain's calm planes. If I quietened it, that half of the picture would go flat.
- **Small pine's crown:** last sitting's repair still works. Its lit top agrees with the big pine about where the sun is.
- **Shadowed road:** green and ochre patches already break up its edges, so there's no ruled line to fix.
- **Houses:** I still accept their crisp edges as part of the picture's plain, built quality.

My heart is happy with it, and I can't see anything I'd enjoy taking further. More marks now would just be fiddling. The journal has this sitting's judgements and the handoff, with the practical lessons that still apply:
- broken patch strokes laid over dry paint
- breaking up straight edges by working across them
- what a rag can and can't lift
- keeping foreground lights warm rather than chalky
- three shadow layers on pale walls
- always masking out neighbouring objects
