# The painter's last reply

I've called the painting **Low Water, Evening Glow**.

It's an estuary at low tide in the evening. The sky grades from blue through violet bars of cloud down to a gold glow on the horizon. A violet headland slopes in from the left and a pale channel winds between two mud banks toward the light. Old posts get smaller as they go back along the left bank. On the right bank a red dinghy is tied by its rope to a stake. Small blue pools in the foreground catch the sky, and four gulls cross the glow.

**Unresolved passages:**
- **Foreground pools:** they're still a flat, too-saturated blue. A light rose glaze barely changed them. A warmer, heavier glaze on the small bottom-left pool turned it a dull, striped tan. I've left them blue as reflections of the sky.
- **Flats and bank tops:** the far flats show horizontal stripes from the row strokes, and the bank tops are fairly straight.
- **Pink sheen bars:** the three in the middle distance read as flat rectangles.
- **Headland base:** the bottom edge is a hard straight line. A faint reflection glaze below it softens it a little.
- **Signature:** the painting isn't signed.

**Practical lessons:**
- **Even washes:** a brush lets out most of its load at the start of a stroke. Topping it up to a small fixed load every 10–25 cells gave even washes.
- **Wet-in-wet:** wetting the sky first with a grid of 5 µL drops let the colour bands blend softly and stay in place. 22 µL drops flooded the sheet and pushed the pigment to the edge of the wet area.
- **Light marks over dark mud:** buff-titanium and Naples yellow only show at heavy loads (0.6–0.8). At 0.1–0.15 they disappear.
- **Thin dark lines:** they need strong sepia and ultramarine (about 0.25 each). Weaker mixes vanished on the mud.
- **Scratch sheet:** one very large trial chunk timed out. After that every scratch call failed with "File exists (os error 17)", even when I started a fresh sheet. I tried the later mixes in hidden corners of the painting instead. Keep chunks small.

The full handoff in `notes/journal.md` has the load and recipe details, and marks which broader rules are only hypotheses.
