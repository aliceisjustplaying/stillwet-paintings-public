**Title:** *Winter Evening by a Frozen Pond, with a Wayside Cross*

**The picture:** a tall, clear evening sky, cold blue at the top shading to a warm band at the horizon, with a thin new moon low over the glow. Across a frozen pond there's a far bank with a low bare wood, four pollard willows, a bare oak with crows and a village church in the haze. In front, a woman in a dark cloak stands with her back to us beside a leaning wooden cross, and her footprints lead up the snow to her. Dry grass stands in the near corners, and the whole picture darkens toward its edges.

I hand-wrote every motif: the oak's own branching function, the pollard willows, the cross, the figure, grass tufts, footprints and the moon as one pointed stroke.

**Files:**
- Painting source: `paintings/lua/frozen_pond.lua`
- Renders: `notes/round8/arm1/painting_1000.png` and `notes/round8/arm1/painting_3200.png`
- Notes (composition, stage-by-stage method and 11 friction points): `notes/round8/arm1/notes.md`

**Top five friction points:**
1. **A lost edge paints over small shapes.** A body brush with `edge="lost"` painted straight over the woman and the cross, even though both were cut out of the mask and the mask read 0 there. The overrun grows with brush width, so any hole smaller than about two brush widths gets filled. A found edge near the figures still nibbled the 5-unit upright, so I glazed the ice instead.
2. **Lightening is nearly impossible; only glazes darken reliably.** A pale scumble over a shadow laid solid white blobs, and a darker, bluer color shift (`color_over`) came out lighter and bright blue. Every shadow ended up as a glaze over dry paint, so a shadow can never be worked wet into wet with the thing that casts it.
3. **A filbert ploughs its paint into rims.** On the far hills each stroke came out as a gray ring, 399 µm of paint on the edges against 11 µm inside, with the sky showing through. I undid it and used the default tool.
4. **Small things are mush at 1000px, and checking them at 3200 is slow.** The moon painted as a mask came out a boomerang, 3-unit crows were invisible and footprints were a few pixels. The first 3200px look cost an 84-second replay. The fix for the moon was one stroke of a pointed brush along the arc.
5. **The underdrawing is invisible at 1000px on this ground**, so I placed everything from coordinates rather than by eye. Also, `easel show N` output can't be fed back to `easel edit N` because of its chunk header line; I stripped it by hand.

Weak spots I'd still work on:
- The drifted snow lines on the ice are hard-ended dashes at 3200.
- The folds in the cloak read a little like pinstripes.
- The crows are close to generic "V" marks.
