**The picture: "Ploughed field before Greifswald, October morning."** A dark ploughed field rises to a crest that dips into a saddle. Beyond it, a Baltic town's towers stand out of the lowland mist in the lightest band of the sky: a slim spire, a tall tower with bulbs and a needle, and a squat tower. A cart track, with two ruts and a grassy hump, winds up the field to the saddle. On the right is a heap of field stones with long shadows from the low sun. There are crows on the furrows, a loose skein of cranes high on the right, and a pale sky over everything.

I deliberately left out the motifs the workshop notebook says every earlier painter reached for: no moon, no figure seen from behind, no dead oak. The furrows are drawn one by one along the ground. Each is traced in meters, projected and painted in pieces, with a smaller brush the farther up the rise it is.

**Top five friction points:**
1. **Linking broke partway through.** Linking started failing with "You have not agreed to the Xcode license agreements." Every build since uses `DEVELOPER_DIR=/Library/Developer/CommandLineTools`. That's the machine, not the engine, but it stops all work.
2. **No way to point strokes along a direction on the ground.** `Handling::angle` only takes a canvas angle. So for the furrows and the track I wrote my own `along(x, y)`: find the ground point, step along the heading, project both points and take the angle. It runs a ray march for every stroke.
3. **Brush size can't change with depth.** A handling has one tool, so the field had to be cut into four depth bands, each with its own brush size. The workshop notebook's complaint that "the stroke size still never changed with depth" still holds.
4. **Long receding lines needed hand-written drags.** `work` places strokes at random, which can't make hundreds of long lines in perspective, and the first attempt came out as brown confetti. So I drew about 140 furrows as serial drags and swapped `held.tool` for each width. That meant writing pressure, swell and gaps by hand, since none of the handling's hand-like behavior comes with a drag.
5. **Ground-aware strokes are slow, and checkpoints go stale easily.** The "track" stage takes about 60 s and "field" about 34 s. I suspect the ray march that my color and angle functions call for every stroke; resuming from a later stage takes only 6 s. Also, any edit to a helper outside `main` marks the sky checkpoint stale, so I sometimes overrode it with `--stale-ok` when I knew the sky hadn't changed.

My main criticisms of the result: the foreground is still mostly one kind of mark repeated, the right side of the middle distance is dull, the rut puddles are still flat pale slips, and the sky is calm to the edge of looking empty.
