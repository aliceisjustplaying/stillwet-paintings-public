## Problems in the revisions

### N1 — new mask paragraph is non-steering but factually wrong

**Should fix in all guides.** Sargent L193–199, Inness/Alma-Tadema L183–189, Tonn L184–190:

> “`work` starts every stroke inside the mask.”
>
> “`clip=true`, `clip=` a mask, or `edge=` (below) stop them at the edge.”

The first statement confuses the stroke's region anchor with its path start. In the specified source revision, `crates/paint/src/handling.rs:430–492` admits clipped strokes seeded outside the region, strokes carried in from outside and paths around an interior center. A path can start outside even when its pigment/field anchor is inside.

The last statement incorrectly equates three controls. `clip=true` uses the passage mask. A mask supplied as `clip=` can have a different extent: it need not stop at the passage's edge. `edge=` creates a fence with variable overrun and thinning; it can paint beyond the original region. See `crates/easel/src/api.rs:867–882`, `:955–970` and `crates/paint/src/fence.rs:1–35,116–128`. The guide itself contradicts the new paragraph two lines later: S L203–204 / I,A L193–194 / T L194–195 says a lost edge “runs well past the line and dissolves.”

The defaults are substantially right: `detail` and `blend` are clipped by default; glaze has default lengths 120–300 units (`crates/paint/src/style.rs:288–301,327–340,353–365`). State these as defaults because options can override them. Stroke width also affects spill; a length alone is not a universal bound.

**Suggested replacement:** “A `work` passage uses its mask to plan and anchor […]”

This documents interface consequences without recommending crisp or soft edges. It is not an aesthetic-contamination finding. The same source evidence makes studio notes L69–72 (“the mask's value only decides where strokes start”) inaccurate; update that sentence with the guide.

### N2 — new capability disclaimer is too broad

**Should fix in all materials notes.** S L288–289 / I L278–279 / A L231–232 / T L282–283:

> “The historical practices above are context, not available controls.”

The concrete exclusions above it are useful and consistent with the exposed interface: linen, pigment-based grounds, one oil share, no separate solvents/resins/driers/varnish and no scraping/abrasion tool. They do not steer a subject or substitute a visual recipe. The final sentence, however, sweeps in supported actions such as brush application, pigment mixing, wet-in-wet work and drying intervals.

**Suggested replacement:** “The unsupported supports, media and removal methods named here are historical context. Available operations are documented in the easel guide.” Add: “Brush wiping unloads the brush; it does not wipe a […]” For Tonn, explicitly add the unsupported projector, adhesive, synthetic-support and ground-binder distinctions identified above. Avoid suggesting ways to imitate the unavailable practices.
