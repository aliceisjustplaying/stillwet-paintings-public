## 3. Whole surface or area by area

- **Lay-in: background as a whole, then shapes area by area** [O]. The background goes on from the top
  down (36 s: a band across the top, working down beside the upper shapes). The darkest patches go in
  as separate spots while much of the background is still bare (40 s). By ~50 s the whole background and
  lower band are covered. Then the light shapes are blocked in **one at a time**. The tallest shape goes
  first (56–64 s), then the far-right shape and the cloth area (~68–76 s), then the large middle shape
  (~80–92 s). The small shapes come last (~92–104 s) [O].
- **After the lay-in: repeated passes over the whole surface**, with some sessions focused on particular
  shapes (sessions 5–7 on pattern and detail) [O].

## 4. Drawing

- [O] He draws freehand at the easel, directly on the canvas, while looking at the setup beside him. No
  projection, grid or transfer is visible.
- [O] The line is dark gray, grainy and slightly soft-edged: charcoal or a similar dry stick (medium [I]).
- [O] Order: first, long horizontals for the table edges and a few landmark marks that set the extent and

- [O] Short dark tick marks sit on the right-hand side edge of the stretched canvas (from 18 s on, still

- [O] The drawn lines stay visible inside the bare light shapes through the whole lay-in (52 s). They
  disappear only when each shape gets painted.

## 5. Bare canvas, ground and what gets covered when

- [O] **No toned ground or imprimatura.** The canvas is bright white when the drawing starts and at 36 s.
- [O] **All the lightest shapes are left as bare white canvas** while the background goes in (by 52 s
  every light shape is still white, with only its contour). They get covered one by one from 56 s to about
  104 s.
- [O] During the block-in the **bare white canvas works as the light value**. The shadow planes and
  mid-tones go on as separate flat strokes, and the lit areas stay white for a while (62 s, 84 s). The
  lightest shape at the top of the picture is still bare at 84–100 s.
- [O] The top tacking edge of the canvas stays white to the end. The right tacking edge was painted over
  with the background paint during the lay-in.

## 6. Edges

- [O] **Lay-in: the background is painted up to the drawn contours**, cutting around the light shapes.
  The boundary is hard and a little ragged where dry paint catches the canvas weave (40 s, 52 s).
- [O] Block-in edges between the shapes and the background stay hard (84 s).
- [O] By ~140–200 s many shadow-side edges have softened into the background, and the transitions inside
  shapes are blended (100 s onward). [I] So later passes overlap the edge from one side or both instead of
  painting up to it.
- [O] The final picture mixes crisp edges on the lit sides and details with soft, lost edges on the shadow
  sides and on the soft dark patches in the background.

## 7. Paint thickness over time

- [O] Lay-in paint is **opaque from the first stroke**. There's no thin wash or turpsy stage. At 36 s the
  background strokes cover the white fully in the middle, and the stroke edges break into dry-brush over
  the canvas weave. [I] The paint was fairly stiff and moderately thin, not diluted.
- [O] The darkest accents at 40 s are solid, flat and opaque.
- [O] From ~100 s on, the lighter passes on the shapes and the background have a **granular, broken
  texture** where paint catches the tops of the weave. [I] Stiff paint was dragged or scumbled over the
  dry lay-in.
- [O] Late passes (330 s) put noticeably **loaded light paint** on the brush tip for the lightest accents.
  [I] The final lights are the thickest paint on the canvas.
- [O] No heavy impasto, knife texture or visible glazing is seen at any point. The final surface still shows
  the weave in the background.

## 11. First passes vs last passes

- **First:** a large-ish flat bristle brush, opaque stiff paint and big flat value masses. The background is
  cut around bare shapes, the darkest spots go in first, shapes are rendered as 2–3 flat value planes with

- **Last:** small brushes, including a long thin one, with dragged or scumbled granular passes over the
  dry paint. There are fine lines and pattern, loaded light accents on the lightest shapes, softened
  shadow-side edges and a background refined but left textured, not smoothed. [O, texture mechanism I]

## 12. Comparison with `notes/research/tonn_materials.md` (§3, §6, §7)

### Confirms
- **§3 "Drawing on the canvas"** ([SEQ22] charcoal on canvas; "that never stops" [IMPR]): he draws a line
  drawing directly on the canvas with a dry, charcoal-like stick and revises it during the drawing session.
  The medium is [I] but consistent.
- **§6 "Direct by default"** ([UP22], [DIR26]): there's no monochrome underpainting and no wash stage. Opaque
  paint at roughly final values goes on from the first background stroke.
- **§6 "Whole surface at once… tightening and refining each time; some details follow the initial
  block-in"** ([IMPR], [PAT20]): after the lay-in, every session revisits the whole surface. Pattern and small
  details come several sessions after the block-in.
- **§6 "Stiff paint"** ([BR22]) and **"not the heaviest painter"** ([VAR21]): the dry-brush broken edges and the
  granular scumbled texture fit stiff paint. No impasto is visible.
- **§6 "Brushes": flats of natural bristle, ~¼–½ in** ([BR21], [MAT23], [BR22]): the brushes that can be
  identified are small flats or filberts of pale bristle within that range. The one dark-handled small brush
  may be the "5% nylon" for small marks (hairs [I]).
- **§7 "From life"** ([ICK], [PROJ]): the setup sits right beside the easel through all sessions.

### Adds
- **§3:** in this case there's **no projection or transfer**. He draws freehand, at painting scale and from
  life. There are no studies or preparatory drawing (none shown). The drawing sequence is landmarks and long
  horizontals, then contours shape by shape, contour only with no value. He puts tick marks on the
  canvas side edge.
- **§3/§6:** **white ground, no imprimatura.** The light shapes are kept as bare white canvas while the
  background goes in, and the white works as the light value during the block-in. The note mentions a
  "brownish imprimatura" only for single-layer studies [AP24; AP26].
- **§6:** lay-in order: background from the top down plus the darkest accents first, cut around the light
  shapes. Then the light shapes are blocked in **one at a time** as flat value planes. So the start works
  area by area, and it turns into whole-surface passes only after the canvas is covered.
- **§6:** edges are hard in the lay-in and softened later. The final lights are the most loaded paint.
- **§7 Duration / sessions:** about **8 sessions** for one small still life (from garment changes [I]).
  About 27% of the video goes to drawing and lay-in and about 70% to refinement.
- **§7 Reworking:** he returns to areas many times. Some areas stay deliberately unmodeled (a flat
  silhouette) for several sessions.

### Contradicts or doesn't fit
- **Nothing contradicts the note outright.** Two points of tension:
  - **§6 "Four stages (layered works)… thin first painting… final glazes" [SEQ22]:** nothing here looks like
    a *thin* first painting or a final glaze. The first paint is opaque, and no transparent late layer is
    visible. That fits the note's own caveat ("idealized… often strayed from") and "direct by default."
    Glazes can't be ruled out at this resolution.
  - **§6 "Single-layer studies… one wet layer… about three hours" [AP24; AP26]:** this piece is small and
    study-like, but it's clearly **multi-session** (about 8 garment changes) over a white ground, not one
    wet layer over an imprimatura. So the note's study pattern isn't the only one he uses for small works.
- **§3 "Transfer by projector" [PROJ; TRAD26]:** it isn't used here. That doesn't contradict the note, which
  ties projection to paintings developed from detailed preparatory drawings.
- **§6 "Palette… arm palette or pochade palette, groups colors by hue" [MAT23; PAL20]:** neither confirmed nor
  contradicted, because the palette is never visible.
- **§6 "Surface correction" (cuttlefish bone, knife) [TEX-B]:** not observed.
- **§7 "Light… clamp light on the easel" [LIGHT26]:** a hanging fitting above the easel may be a light
  [I, unconfirmed].
