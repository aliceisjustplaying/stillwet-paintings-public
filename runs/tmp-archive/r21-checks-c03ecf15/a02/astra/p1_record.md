## Mixing piles
- Thin paint over the warm ground appeared lighter and more muted than the opaque swatches.
- A dark pile stippled over paint of nearly the same full-strength tone was almost invisible.
- Mixtures matched by eye in thick swatches did not match the appearance of an existing thin film.

## Strokes
- A body pass at coverage 2.4 covered the underlying paint instead of leaving sparse marks; coverage counted overlapping layers.
- A body pass at coverage 1.1 with fill=false and varying load left conspicuous broken patches.
- Reducing the load to 0.3 did not make the dark body paint fade; it still hid the paint beneath it.
- A full body coat over dry paint covered unwanted stippling and matched the surrounding dark paint.

## Blending
- Broad passes at coverage 3 with fill=true remained visibly separate until one blending pass joined them into a smooth gradation.
- Blending wet darker paint with neighboring pale paint lightened the darker passage and reduced the value difference.
- Badger blending left blotches and weave-dot speckle where it lifted paint.
- Blending many separate flecks left visible dashes and soft blotches rather than an even film.
- Blending adjacent wet body colors mixed them and spread their edges.

## Wet into wet
- Blending across fresh dark paint carried it into a gray smear through the adjoining pale paint.
- A badger carried wet dark paint across an edge even when the supplied mask excluded the neighboring passage.
- A glaze brush picked up wet dark paint and dragged it into long smears.
- Dark paint laid over wet paler paint came out lighter than its pile.
- Painting over wet dark marks left traces of the previous marks visible.

## Glazing
- Unclipped glaze strokes extended beyond the supplied mask and retained visible bristle texture.
- Lead-white glazes lightened and obscured underlying dark paint instead of merely tinting it.
- Medium-rich transparent glazes tinted the underlying pale paint without the same chalky covering effect.
- An unblended pale glaze left blotchy pools at the loaded stroke starts.
- A pale glaze at coverage 1.8 and loads up to 0.85 largely covered the underlying paint.
- Dark glazing over dark paint left mottling and a visible boundary rather than an even fade.
- A blurred mask and a load that rose then fell did not remove the glaze's mottling or visible rim.

## Stippling
- Width-6 stippling at coverage 1 left distinct touches with fine weave texture visible within them.
- Dragged stippling left thin, distinct marks when it was not blended afterward.
- Pale stippling over darker paint left conspicuous bright dots.
- Grading the density of dark stippling still produced a visibly darker patch rather than an invisible transition.

## Drying
- Opaque repainting on dry paint covered marks that had remained visible through wet-on-wet corrections.

## Masks and edges
- Opaque paint covered fine marks omitted from an exclusion mask.
- Glazes veiled marks omitted from an exclusion mask even when larger neighboring marks were protected.
- A clipped blending pass left a sharp boundary where the blended paint differed from the paint outside the mask.
- Applying a slightly different dark mixture through a limited mask left a visible tone step at its boundary.
- Blurring a mask extended paint coverage beyond its original edge.
- Dark paint applied through a blurred clip mask still produced a clearly visible edge.
- Roughening a mask produced a scalloped painted edge.
- lose() left an unintended pale mark beyond the edge.

## Brushes
- Flat-brush strokes from one load came out thin and hard-edged.
- Round-brush strokes spread into separate bristle traces.
- Rigger strokes with pressure falling to zero tapered to points.
