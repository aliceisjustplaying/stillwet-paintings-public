- day 1, 09:00: # Journal - Sitting 1

  ## Vision & Composition
  - Subject: A rugged Western frontier cowboy blasting a shambling, desiccated zombie at near point-blank range.
  - Atmosphere: A desolate rocky canyon trail under a cold moonlit night, violently split open by an explosive golden-orange muzzle blast.
  - Value architecture:
    - Strong, stark chiaroscuro.
    - Deep cool nocturnal background (ultramarine, bone black, raw umber).
    - High-intensity warm focal core at (x ≈ 480..560, y ≈ 380..440) where the blast detonates.
    - Cowboy (left, x ≈ 150..420, y ≈ 250..720) caught in sharp rim light from the right, deep cast shadow on back and under hat brim.
    - Zombie (right, x ≈ 580..880, y ≈ 220..710) violently thrown back or lunging forward into the blast, face illuminated in sickly decaying relief (greens, earths, raw flesh, rotted garments).
  - Substrate: Toned warm ground (lead white 10, raw umber 1.8, yellow ochre 0.8; 45 µm brush-applied on 18x14 linen). Dry.

  ## Hypotheses & Material Strategy
  - Hypothesis 1: A lean turpentine lay-in (`turps=0.5..0.6`) of raw umber and bone black provides rapid-drying, transparent-to-semi-opaque darks that preserve luminous undertones without beading or heavy impasto.
  - Hypothesis 2: Establishing the blast illumination early as a warm wash will allow subsequent cool glazes and impasto highlights to create dramatic depth.

- day 1, 10:27: ## Handoff & Sitting 1 Reflections

  ### Work Accomplished
  - Conceived, drew, and executed *Lead and Brimstone at Deadwood Ridge*: a high-impact narrative painting of a frontier cowboy blasting a lunging zombie at point-blank range.
  - Executed in four distinct physical campaigns:
    1. Underdrawing with charcoal and 2H/HB lead pencil on dry toned ground (lead white 10, raw umber 1.8, yellow ochre 0.8; 45 µm). Fixed.
    2. Lean lay-in (`turps=0.5..0.55`) blocking nocturnal sky, distant mesas, desert ground, cowboy silhouette, zombie mass, and reserved light zones.
    3. Body and impasto modeling (`lay=1..6`): high-relief muzzle detonation core, blast rim-lighting, cool moonlight rim, zombie skull/maw/shredded chest anatomy, and spatter effects.
    4. Final integration: directional cast shadows radiating away from the blast, illuminated desert stones/gravel, moonlit mesa crest, brass cartridge belt, spur glint, eye catchlight, curling gunsmoke, and artist signature.

  ### Passages Observed & Resolved
  - The dual-light system (incandescent golden-orange blast vs. cool silvery desert moonlight) produces immense spatial depth and chiaroscuro tension.
  - The physical relief of the blast core (`lay=6`, cadmium yellow + lead white) projects tangibly from the canvas surface, as verified under raking relief light.
  - Directional cast shadows and rake-lit pebbles firmly anchor both figures into the desert ground plane.

  ### Practical Lessons & Material Recipes
  - *Lean Reserves*: Applying dark solvent washes directly under subsequent brilliant lights results in muddy pickup. Reserving high-key areas cleanly on the toned ground preserves pigment purity and vibrance.
  - *Spatter Dynamics*: `b:spatter` with high-load, fluid mixes (`p_blast_core_hot` and `p_gore_dark`) effectively renders ballistic debris, flying sparks, and gore with organic variation in trajectory and droplet size.
  - *Impasto for Detonation*: Stiff paint with high lead white and cadmium content holds bristle ridges and sharp edges without slumping, creating a palpable physical source of illumination.
  - *No failed recipes in this sitting*: all three scratch trials accurately predicted the performance of the mixes on the canvas.

  ### Passages for Next Sitting Review
  - Review the dry/setting surface across the whole canvas.
  - Confirm balance of values and transitions between the drifting smoke and nocturnal sky in normal light.
