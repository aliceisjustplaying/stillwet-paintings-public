- day 1, 09:04: ## Sitting 1 — Preparation, Underdrawing, and Scratch Trials for Sky, Water & Foreground

  ### Composition and Order
  - **Canvas**: 620 × 450 mm (`aspect = 1.38`, `W = 1000, H = 725`), plain-weave Dresden linen (`14 × 13` threads/cm), prepared with three layers in walnut oil: two knife-leveled warm earth/ochre lower grounds (`35 µm` red earth/yellow ochre/lead white; `25 µm` lead white/yellow ochre/raw umber/red earth) and a brush-applied warm whitish top ground (`20 µm` lead white 12 / yellow ochre 0.5 / raw umber 0.2 / vermilion 0.05) whose diagonal bristle striations subtly texture the surface.
  - **Geometric Structure**: Severe central vertical axis (`x = 500`) and dead-level ruled horizon (`y = 485`, giving 67% of the canvas to the sky). Four elements only:
    1. Banded twilight sky after sunset with a small crescent moon (`x = 515, y = 152`) partly veiled inside an upward-opening arch of evening cloud.
    2. Calm bodden/sea (`y = 485..614`) echoing the sky's bands more transparently, framed at the horizon (`x = 0..378` and `x = 622..1000`) by two low mist-veiled headlands that leave an open channel of bright water on the axis (`x = 378..622`).
    3. Shallow dark rocky promontory (`y = 575..725`) parallel to the picture plane, peaking on the axis (`x = 500, y = 575`) with flanking angular glacial boulders, cutting the near completely from the far.
    4. Solitary ancient retrenched winter oak (*Quercus robur*) rooted on the apex of the promontory (`x = 500, y = 584`), its wide hollow-fissured trunk crossing the horizon and dividing at `y = 375` into a central dead stag-head spire and two overarching gnarled primary boughs that form an upward-opening vault around the central sky and moon.

  ### Material Trials on Scratch Canvas (Substrate: Dry 3-layer walnut-oil brush-striated ground over fixed 2H/HB graphite)
  1. **Failed Recipe — `trial_zenith_prussian`**:
     - *Conditions*: Substrate dry 3-layer ground; recipe `Prussian blue 2.5 : bone black 1.2 : lead white 2.0` in walnut oil with `turps = 0.65`; tool `filbert 18` (`hand = "broad"`), `load = 0.75`, `coverage = 1.2`, `fill = false`.
     - *Observed result*: Produced a harsh, cold greenish-petrol slab that broke the twilight tonality; `turps = 0.65` without `fill = true` left streaky gaps where the pale ground glared through at stroke ends.
     - *Decision*: Rejected. Will not use Prussian blue + bone black for the open sky vault.
  2. **Failed Handling — Unblended post-blend stippling (`sky_rose` over `sky_mid` zone)**:
     - *Conditions*: Substrate `"open"` blended sky film (`thinner = 0.28..0.30`); recipe `sky_rose` (`lead white 7.0 : Naples yellow 1.8 : rose madder 0.55 : vermilion 0.14 : pale smalt 0.9`, `thinner = 0.28`); `stipple` (`width = 2.5, coverage = 0.65, pressure = {0.25, 0.45}, feather = 0.5, dips = default 24`) applied *after* the horizontal `blend` pass.
     - *Observed result*: Left visible patchy clusters of warm pink dots where `sky_rose` overlapped the cooler `sky_mid` zone (`y = 260..320`), disrupting the atmospheric unity.
     - *Hypothesis*: Stippling a single contrasting pile over an already-blended open gradient without a subsequent blender pass (or without matching the local gradient weight) creates visible 24-touch dip patches rather than an atmospheric veil.
  3. **Successful Trial — Graded Smalt/Cobalt/Rose/Naples-Yellow Sky (`Patch B` left half)**:
     - *Conditions*: Substrate dry 3-layer ground; four walnut-oil piles thinned with `thinner = 0.25..0.30`:
       - `sky_zenith`: `smalt 5.5 : cobalt blue 2.2 : lead white 2.2 : rose madder 0.45 : raw umber 0.3` (`thinner = 0.30`)
       - `sky_mid`: `lead white 5.5 : pale smalt 4.0 : smalt 1.6 : rose madder 0.38 : yellow ochre 0.22` (`thinner = 0.30`)
       - `sky_rose`: `lead white 7.0 : Naples yellow 1.8 : rose madder 0.55 : vermilion 0.14 : pale smalt 0.9` (`thinner = 0.28`)
       - `sky_horizon`: `lead white 8.5 : Naples yellow 3.8 : yellow ochre 1.1 : vermilion 0.16` (`thinner = 0.25`)
     - *Handling*: `work` (`hand = "broad", tool = "filbert 20", angle = 0, coverage = 1.6, fill = true, load = 0.82`) with continuous vertical `piles` weighting, followed by a single horizontal `blend` pass (`angle = 0`) while `"open"`.
     - *Observed result*: Seamless, luminous horizontal twilight gradation with subtle micro-pooling in the brushed ground striations; fixed HB underdrawing lines remain faintly visible under the thin film (`~8–12 µm` after solvent evaporation), allowing accurate placement of the oak in the next campaign.
  4. **Successful Trial — Three-Zone Calm Water (`Patch C`) & Lean Earth Foreground Underpainting (`Patch D`)**:
     - *Conditions*: `water_horizon` (`smalt 3.8 : pale smalt 2.5 : lead white 2.6 : raw umber 0.9 : rose madder 0.35`), `water_echo` (`lead white 5.0 : Naples yellow 2.2 : yellow ochre 1.0 : rose madder 0.45 : pale smalt 1.4 : raw umber 0.45`), and `water_near` (`smalt 4.2 : raw umber 2.2 : lead white 1.6 : bone black 0.45 : yellow ochre 0.5`), each with `oil = "walnut", medium = 0.25, thinner = 0.25`, laid with `filbert 14`, `angle = 0, coverage = 1.5, fill = true, load = 0.78` and blended once horizontally (`angle = 0`). Foreground underpainting `fg_under` (`raw umber 4.5 : bone black 2.0 : yellow ochre 1.5 : red earth 0.8, oil = "walnut", turps = 0.45`) laid with `filbert 12, coverage = 1.4, fill = true`.
     - *Observed result*: Water reads as calm, deeper and more transparent than the sky while echoing its warm middle glow; foreground underlayer provides a fast-drying, warm-dark earth foundation.
     - *Uncertainties*: How much the single horizontal `blend` pass over the full sky width (`W = 1000`) will lift paint at the top and bottom edges of the sky mask (`y = 0` and `y = 485`), and whether the cloud arch and veiled crescent moon integrate better when laid wet-into-wet before blending or glazed/scumbled after the first sky layer sets.
