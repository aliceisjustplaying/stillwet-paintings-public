- day 1, 09:08: Rehearsal 1 (scratch) findings: (1) `work`/`stipple` nested option tables take POSITIONAL values — `drag={length=7, angle=0}` and `dips={touches=18, load=0.5}` raise "error converting Lua nil to f32"; write `drag={7, 0}`, `dips={18, 0.5}`. (2) `broken=true` is invalid — `broken` is an amount (number); `broken=true` raises "error converting Lua boolean to f32". (3) Scratch canvas has its own variable namespace: painting globals are nil there; knife piles fresh (use locals to avoid clobbering). (4) Graded `piles={{p1, fn}, ...}` with function weights works well for gradients. (5) Picture problems seen: sky pale zone too large/light so the afterglow band does not read as brightest; water too light mid-zone and too streaky; figure too thin (reads as a stick); shore ochre pass too loud; tree far too sparse (thin limbs, few twigs — reads as a sapling); far shore strip and mist veil read well; glimmer column reads only in the lower half (pale on pale up top) — darken water sooner below the reflection band.
- day 1, 09:08: Sky solved (rehearsals 7-10). Failed approaches, for the record: (a) full-sky `blend` pass -> cross-hatched weave; (b) `rag:wipe` at pressure 0.4 over the wet sky -> rag saturated (load 1.0), scraped the sky to speckle and a pale lattice; (c) ruler-straight broad strokes alone -> regular corduroy rows; (d) big 64-wide filbert alone -> mechanical scallop mosaic. What works: base = one `work` pass with a custom soft filbert (width 64, stiffness 0.35), ruler=true, angle=0, length {280,420}, coverage 1.15, fill=true, ramps {0.25,0.25}, mix_jitter 0.25, graded `piles` by y (deep->mid->pale->glow, glow weighted by a horizontal gaussian centred x=500); then soft cloud bands (body hand, soft ellipse masks, local `blend` only); then the afterglow glaze (hand="glaze", load_at varying with the same gaussian); THEN smooth the whole wet sky with a clean badger 40 dragged in horizontal passes every ~34 units, two rounds (pressure 0.3 then 0.22), `b:wipe(0.92)` between passes — the clean brush lifts a little wet paint and smears the rest, melting stroke structure into soft horizontal streaks. Result: quiet banded dusk sky, value peak at the afterglow band. Also settled: figure needs a solid dark head (gesture+touch, was reading grey when only touched); shore lights must be heavily muted (ochre 0.22 / shoreDark 0.78, load 0.2) and the wet waterline edge uses the warm grey rim pile, not ochre; tree crown built from a hand-placed branch skeleton (taper via pointed round brushes, pressure 0.85->0.1), twigs along the outer boughs plus drifting tip clusters with occasional forking twigs — starburst-only clusters read as formula. Water: broad hand, pressure {0.35,0.5}, graded piles, bright reflection only in a narrow band under the shore, darkening by y~575.
- day 27, 14:33: The tree saga (campaigns 4-6), lessons for the record. (1) Stiff tube paint (umber+black, no medium) holds every brush's bristle ridges: limbs laid with stiff round brushes read as mechanical hatching; a fluid re-lay (medium 0.3) is too transparent and lets the old ridges show through; an opaque stiff re-lay re-ridges. Accepted: the dry-brush striation along branches as bark character — it reads correctly at picture scale. (2) A long single gesture over a light ground is solid (scratch test) — pale bands on the painting's leader were NOT stroke gaps but my own rim strokes: I offset the rims from the branch centerlines by less than the branches' half-width, so the pale rims wander across the branches' faces and into the sky, some crossing the dark far-shore strip where they read as scratches. (3) Re-laying over paint that is still open drags it: the 4b re-lay and my two leader tests (one treeDark, one treeSoft lay=4) lifted bands of the wet branch paint, and 6b's chunked re-lay over the still-wet thick parts made new marks; the trunk (never re-laid after drying) came out clean. Rule: never re-lay until `drying()` says dry at the thickest points — thick medium-rich films stay tacky for weeks (test 2's lay=4 film was still tacky at day 27). (4) The chunked re-lay (2-3 points per chunk, fresh full load per chunk, lay=2-3) does produce a solid opaque film over dry paint — the trunk and the leader's dry upper part are solid and clean. (5) A rim light must sit ON the light-facing edge (offset ~half the branch width minus 1-2 units, on the edge), not near the centerline. Remaining repair (campaign 6c): overpaint the stray rim lines with sky-matched graded piles (skyPale+glow by y; shoreFar where the L4 rim crosses the far-shore strip), re-lay the leader solid, then lay proper edge rims on the trunk's left edge, the leader's lower-left edge, L2's upper-left edge. Shore checked at 1:1 and left alone — quiet.
